04 - Set Up
Set up

Set up in 5 easy steps

About 10 minutes, no tools needed for a normal desk setup. Mark each step done as you go.

0 of 5 steps done

1

Place the Tiny

Put the Tiny flat on the desk, standing upright, or mount it behind the monitor with the VESA bracket supplied. Keep the side air vents clear - leave about 10 cm of open space around them.

2

Connect the monitor

Take the HDMI cable from the monitor box. Plug one end into the HDMI port on the back of the Tiny (port 8) and the other end into the monitor. Connect the monitor power cable to the wall.

Picture not coming later? This cable is the first thing to check.

3

Connect keyboard and mouse

Plug the keyboard into the rear USB port marked Smart Power On (port 9) - this lets the keyboard switch the PC on, which is very useful when the Tiny is mounted out of reach. Plug the mouse into any other rear USB port. Keep the front ports free for daily use.

4

Connect network and power

For the office network or wired internet, plug the network (LAN) cable into the Ethernet port (port 12) until it clicks. Then connect the 135 W power adapter to the power port (port 5) and to the wall socket. A surge protector or UPS is recommended where available.

5

Switch on and sign in

Switch on the monitor, then press the round power button on the front of the Tiny. Windows 11 Pro will start - follow the on-screen steps, set your sign-in PIN, and let Windows finish its first updates. Your desktop is ready.

06 - How to Use
How to use

Everyday basics - what every user should know

Grouped for easy learning: start with the first group, and the rest will feel easy. No experience needed - just follow the steps on each card.

Animated guide - keyboard shortcuts

Start here - power, sign-in and the screen

1

Switch on, sleep, shut down

Press the round power button on the front to switch on. For short breaks, use Sleep (Start menu, then Power, then Sleep) - it wakes in seconds and your work stays open. Shut down fully at the end of the week, or before moving the PC.

2

Restart is the real refresh

Shut down and Restart are not the same. If the PC ever feels slow or strange, choose Restart - it fully refreshes Windows. It fixes more small problems than anything else in this guide.

3

Sign in with a PIN

Set a short PIN instead of typing a long password: Settings, then Accounts, then Sign-in options, then PIN. Quick for you, hard for anyone else - your sign-in is protected by the TPM security chip inside the PC.

4

Your Windows screen in 1 minute

The Start button (Windows icon, bottom centre) opens your programs. The bar at the bottom is the Taskbar - programs you open appear there. The small icons near the clock (bottom right) show sound, network and time. The empty area is your Desktop - keep only shortcuts you use daily.

5

Find anything in seconds

Press the Windows key on the keyboard and just start typing - a program name, a file name, or a setting like "brightness". Press Enter to open the first result. This is the fastest way to work in Windows 11.

6

Switch between open programs

Hold the Alt key and tap Tab to jump between open programs. Click a program's icon on the Taskbar to bring it forward. Right-click a program icon and choose "Pin to taskbar" for the ones you open every day.

7

Two windows side by side

Drag a window to the left edge of the screen until it snaps to half the screen, then pick another window for the right half. Or press Windows key + Left or Right arrow. Perfect for copying between a document and an email.

8

If the PC freezes

First, wait 30 seconds - it may be busy. Then press Ctrl + Alt + Delete and open Task Manager to close the stuck program. Only if nothing responds at all, hold the power button for about 10 seconds to force it off - unsaved work will be lost, so use this only as the last step.

9

Sign out on a shared PC

If others use the same PC, do not just walk away. Click Start, then your name, then Sign out - or lock instantly with Windows key + L. Your files and email stay private to your account.

Files, pen drives and printing

10

Where your files live

Save work files in Documents, pictures in Pictures - these folders are easy to find in File Explorer (the yellow folder icon). Avoid saving important files on the Desktop; they are harder to back up and easy to lose track of.

11

Plug in USB devices

Pen drives, external disks, printers - just plug them in and Windows sets them up. Use the front ports (2, 3 and 4) for things you connect and remove often, and the rear ports for things that stay connected.

12

Copy files to a pen drive - safely

Plug the drive in, open it in File Explorer, and copy-paste or drag your files across. Wait until copying finishes. Then click the small USB icon near the clock and choose Eject before pulling it out - this protects your files from damage.

13

Take a screenshot

Press Windows key + Shift + S, drag over the area you want, then paste it (Ctrl + V) into an email, Word file or chat. Ideal for showing your IT team exactly what an error message says.

14

Print a document

Press Ctrl + P in your program, choose the printer, and check the page preview before printing. A USB printer usually sets itself up when plugged in. For a new printer: Settings, then Bluetooth and devices, then Printers and scanners, then Add device.

15

Check your free space

Open Settings, then System, then Storage to see how full the 512 GB drive is. If it is nearly full, empty the Recycle Bin and clear old files in Downloads. A drive with some free space keeps the PC fast.

Screen, sound, meetings and connections

16

Adjust your monitor

Tilt the screen up or down (-5 to +22 degrees) until the top of the screen is near eye level. The buttons under the right edge open the monitor menu for brightness and input selection. In Windows, Settings, then System, then Display controls brightness options and Night light, which makes the screen warmer and easier on the eyes in the evening.

17

Make text bigger

If text feels small: Settings, then Accessibility, then Text size - move the slider. Or Settings, then System, then Display, then Scale for bigger text and icons everywhere. The monitor's sharp setting is 1600 x 900 - keep resolution there for the clearest picture.

18

Use two screens

Connect a second monitor to the DisplayPort (port 6). Then press Windows key + P and choose Extend - drag windows from one screen to the other and work on both at once. Choose Duplicate instead when a room needs to see the same screen, for example on a projector or meeting-room display.

19

Sound and meetings

The built-in speaker covers basic sound and alerts. For calls and meetings, plug a headset into the front jack (port 1) - voices are clearer and your microphone is closer. Adjust volume from the speaker icon near the clock, and check the headset is chosen in your meeting app settings.

20

Charge your phone

Use the front USB port 3 (Always On). It charges a phone or tablet even when the PC is switched off - the power adapter just needs to stay connected to the wall.

21

Switch on from the keyboard

If the keyboard is plugged into rear port 9 (Smart Power On), it can power the PC on. Perfect when the Tiny is mounted behind the monitor and the power button is hard to reach.

22

Get connected - wired

Plug the LAN cable into port 12 until it clicks. Wired is the fastest and most stable connection for office systems, shared folders and printers.

23

Get connected - wireless

Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) + Bluetooth 5.2 (Optional). Where wireless is fitted: click the network icon near the clock to join a Wi-Fi network, and use Settings, then Bluetooth and devices, to pair a Bluetooth headset, keyboard or mouse - put the device in pairing mode first.

24

Save your work - Ctrl + S

The simplest power-user habit: press Ctrl + S often while you work. In Microsoft 365 programs, turn on AutoSave for files saved in OneDrive or SharePoint, and the program saves for you as you type.

Keep it fast

Restart once a week

Sleep is fine day to day, but a full Restart once a week clears memory, finishes updates and keeps Windows feeling new. Friday before you leave is a good routine.

Close what you finish

Programs left open all week quietly use memory. Close the ones you are done with - your open work will thank you with speed.

Keep space free

Try to keep at least 10 percent of the drive free. Clear Downloads monthly, empty the Recycle Bin, and turn on Storage Sense (Settings, then System, then Storage) so Windows cleans temporary files by itself.

One home for every file

Save into Documents and its folders, not all over the Desktop. Finding files takes seconds, and backups actually capture them.

Let updates finish

When Windows says it is updating, let it complete - never switch off or unplug during an update, even if it takes a few minutes. Plan restarts for lunch or end of day.

Pin your daily programs

Right-click your most-used programs and pin them to the Taskbar or Start. Fewer searches, fewer clicks, faster mornings.

Keep it safe

Lock when you leave

Press Windows key + L every single time you step away - even for a minute. One second, and your work, email and files stay private.

Guard your PIN and password

Never share them, never write them on a note stuck to the monitor. Your IT team will never ask for your password by phone or email - anyone who does is not your IT team.

Pause before you click

Unexpected email attachments, "your account is locked" warnings and prize messages are the most common trap. Check the sender's real address, and if a message pushes you to hurry, ask your IT team first.

Be careful with pen drives

Only use drives you know and trust. Unknown drives are a classic way infections spread - this PC's Smart USB Protection helps, but your care is the first defence.

Install only trusted software

Use programs provided by your office or from the maker's official site. Free download sites bundle unwanted extras that slow PCs down.

Back up what matters

Save important files to your office network drive, OneDrive, or an external disk - not only on this PC. The SSD inside is fast, but a second copy is what saves you if anything ever goes wrong.

Keep it healthy

Give it air

Never cover the side vents or keep the Tiny inside a closed cupboard. Leave about 10 cm of open space around the vents - a cool PC is a quiet, long-lasting PC.

Dust it monthly

Dust slowly blocks vents and fans. Once a month, with the PC off, wipe the vents and the top with a dry cloth or a soft brush.

Protect the power

Connect through a surge protector or a UPS where you can. It guards the PC against power cuts and voltage spikes, and a UPS gives you time to save and shut down properly.

Mind the lightning

During heavy lightning, finish your work, shut down, and switch off at the wall if you can. It is the simplest protection against a power surge.

Front for quick, rear for keeps

Front ports for pen drives and charging - things that come and go. Rear ports for the keyboard, mouse and printer - things that stay. Your desk stays tidy and cables stop getting knocked loose.

Label before you move

Changing desks? Put a small tape label on each cable before unplugging. Reconnecting takes two minutes instead of twenty - and use the Ports section of this guide as your map.

Signing in - good to know

  • The licence lives on the Microsoft account, not on the PC. Signing in with the wrong account will not activate Office.
  • The PC must be online the first time - activation talks to Microsoft.
  • Wrong account signed in? On the same Account page, click Sign out, then sign in with the correct one.

Product key - good to know

  • The key has 25 characters in 5 groups of 5, like XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX.
  • Find it on the licence card or in the email from your supplier. Keep it safe and do not share it.
  • You can also redeem a new key in the browser at office.com/setup, signed in with your Microsoft account.

Getting service

This set was supplied by Abans PLC. For service, contact Abans with your invoice and the serial number from the label on the unit - that number identifies your exact configuration (MTM 12THS0EW00).

Before you call

Try the Help section above first - most issues are a cable or a setting. Note down what happened and any message on the screen; it helps the service team fix things faster.
